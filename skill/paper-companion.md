# Paper companions

What differs for a paper companion. Everything else is in `STYLE.md`, which holds for every
report: its teaching rules (prose that weaves the items into a narrative, recaps, "In words"
folds, figures, plots and tables, examples) apply here in full.

Pc1. **Order and scope.** The text follows the paper's order. Its scope is the paper, or the part
of it that RMB's request names. The paper's statements that the companion reports are nodes of
the outline by definition (H3); explanations, steps, figures and examples are added to them.

Pc2. **Opening.** After the opening of M1, a section "Big picture" with a map of the core items.

Pc3. **The paper's version beside the report's.** The paper's equations are printed as printed,
with section and page, next to the report's own version, with a term-by-term map of the
notation.

Pc4. **Status lines.** After each statement, a line that says what it is ("Say what every piece
is" in `STYLE.md`), with these words: *Known* (cited, with a link; not shown again), *Reminder*
(the steps follow the original's steps), *Known for new objects*, *Second route* (with what it
adds), *New here*. The convention is stated once in the reading notes; an item without a status
line is new on the page.

Pc5. **What is the paper's.** Every report says what is the paper's and what is the report's. In
a paper summary every item that reports the paper has a status line "*Paper, §2.2* (p. 468):
defined."; in any other paper companion, every line that prints or reports a paper's statement
("*Paper, Eq. 3* (§2.2, p. 468): defined. As printed:") carries the verb too, and the report
states the verbs it uses next to its status-line convention. The verb says what the paper does
with the statement and comes from this closed list:
- *assumed*: a model assumption;
- *defined*: an object, a model, a notation or a design;
- *stated*: claimed without an argument;
- *argued*: claimed with an argument shorter than a derivation (say how long);
- *quoted* (source): taken from the named source, not derived;
- *derived*: derived in the paper;
- *found* (simulation or data): a result of the paper's simulation or data analysis;
- *judged*: the paper's interpretation, attribution or recommendation;
- *described*: data, aims, a plan or an algorithm, with no claim.

An item that reports several things gives one verb per part. After the verb, *Derived in* links a
report that derives the statement, *Explained in* a report that explains what it means without
deriving it. Slips in the paper and the report's commentary go in sections of their own; their
items' status lines start with *Own commentary*, followed by the five words of Pc4.

Pc6. **Plain-words folds.** Each folding section has, right after its gist, a plain-words fold
(`::: plain`, "In plain words"): what the section does and why, in everyday words, with a
concrete example or an analogy and almost no notation. It is written for a reader who has not
read the section, and it may say where the analogy fails. It never replaces a definition; the
section still states everything precisely. "Plain words only" in the toolbar then shows only the
opening, the section titles, their takeaways and these folds, so the whole report reads in a few
minutes as a way in. Write it after the section, so it says what the section really does.

Pc7. **R code** folds away (`STYLE.md`, "Form of a report"; E4).

Pc8. **Final section** of results used without proof (K1): "Known results", boxes numbered K.1,
K.2, ...

Pc9. **Paper summaries** take the same form.
