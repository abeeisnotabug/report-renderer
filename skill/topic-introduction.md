# Topic introductions

What differs for a topic introduction. Everything else is in `STYLE.md`, which holds for every
report. How one is made (outline, writers, assembler): `SKILL.md`, "Making a topic introduction".

Tq1. **The question.** A topic introduction introduces a topic so that a reader who does not know
it yet can follow, and reads like a textbook chapter. Most topics can be stated as a question
("when does a process exist, given its finite-dimensional distributions?"), and most questions
name a topic, so the text speaks of "the question" for either. If RMB names no question, the
writer states the purpose of the text as one and shows it in the opening (M1).

Tq2. **Order.** The outline's tree (H1) is the question, its sub-questions in the order they are
answered, and below them the sections and paragraphs. Every element, the source's material
included, must serve a node of it (H3).

Tq3. **Status as one word in the item title**, in the brackets where other types put [core]:
assumed, defined, known, derived, quoted ("**X3. [derived] Result: title.**"); a side trip keeps
"[side trip]". No status lines. Open: whether the one word is enough is judged on the first
pages.

Tq4. **No plain-words folds** (`::: plain`): the "In words" folds (P7) are the L1 layer, and the
"Plain words only" button does not appear.

Tq5. **No R code on the page** (E4).

Tq6. **Final section** of results used without proof (K1): "Known results", boxes numbered K.1,
K.2, ...
